# Data & trust: research behind three Foam concepts

Reviewed 24 September 2026. This is a design sourcebook for choosing a direction, not proposed website copy or a security assessment.

## What the page needs to do

The current page offers reassurance before explaining the product. The three alternatives put useful information earlier: where figures originate, what an account connection involves, how to interpret metrics, and where to find further detail. The existing Foam miniatures, typography, colours and prominent CTA buttons provide continuity with the rest of the website.

The research below uses official product, help and trust pages. It establishes what those companies publish, not independent proof of their claims or evidence that a particular layout improves conversion. Adaptations are design judgments.

## Patterns worth adopting

**Show the product doing the thing being explained.** [Google Drive](https://workspace.google.com/products/drive/) pairs individual capabilities with simplified product views, feature navigation and clear actions. Its security section illustrates actual controls rather than relying entirely on symbolic reassurance. [Google Workspace’s AI privacy page](https://workspace.google.com/security/ai-privacy/) similarly pairs explanations with relevant settings and links to supporting documentation. For Foam, the miniature should explain a connection, permission or figure. Keep the button close to that explanation. Google’s capabilities and security promises do not transfer to Foam.

**Make the source understandable.** [Modash’s data page](https://www.modash.io/data) starts with a numbered explanation of collection, organisation and analysis, then addresses creators’ and brands’ different concerns. It preserves a commercial next step at the end. Foam can borrow the clear progression without adopting Modash’s public-data discovery model or its claims about database coverage.

**Explain a permission in terms of its consequences.** [CreatorIQ’s creator privacy guide](https://creatorsupport.creatoriq.com/hc/en-us/articles/13576532602637-How-do-we-protect-your-data-privacy) answers practical questions about access before presenting a more detailed permission/use table. This is a better model for Foam’s friendly explanation than a large shield alone. Exact claims about reading messages, posting, account selection or revocation require Foam-specific evidence.

**Treat definitions and limitations as useful information.** [Modash’s demographics guide](https://help.modash.io/en/articles/13715083-understanding-audience-demographics-and-insights) distinguishes estimates from exact quantities and explains missing data. [HypeAuditor’s methodology](https://hypeauditor.com/collect-analyze-influencer-data/) covers sources, processing, variable update frequency and a correction contact. Foam’s page should help readers identify the platform, metric and relevant period, and offer a contact for specific freshness questions. Neither company’s methodology or update schedule describes Foam.

**Layer explanation and evidence.** [Notion’s security page](https://www.notion.com/security) organises its overview into recognisable topics and links to policies, technical information, a trust centre and service status. [Slack’s trust centre](https://slack.com/trust) serves as a concise directory for privacy, security, compliance, AI and data management. [Airtable’s trust page](https://www.airtable.com/company/trust-and-security) separates its programme, product controls, compliance and privacy documents. [Traackr’s trust centre](https://trust.traackr.com/?format=html) provides a focused route to supporting reports and practical FAQs. Foam needs an approachable overview with real policy/contact destinations; it does not need to imitate an enterprise certification catalogue.

## What Foam’s own sources support

[Foam’s overview](https://www.foam.io/overview) describes first-party data from social platform APIs and bringing metrics and platform content into pitching tools. That supports explaining the source, rather than making an unsupported claim of universal accuracy.

[Foam’s media-kit page](https://www.foam.io/media-kits) describes customisable kits combining content, analytics and text in a shareable presentation. That supports the connection between a figure and the creator’s wider story.

The current [Foam homepage](https://www.foam.io/) also positions account connection as a way to enrich available data, rather than a blanket prerequisite for every use. The concepts should preserve that distinction. A reader should not infer that every profile requires the same connection or that every metric is always available.

Although existing Foam marketing uses “real-time” language, these sources do not establish a precise per-platform refresh interval. They also do not establish the complete granular permission scope for each connection. The previews therefore avoid invented timestamps, “LIVE” indicators, guaranteed cadence, blanket read-only/no-post claims, and new security or compliance promises.

## Three different approaches

1. **Source first — `/lab/data-trust/1`.** A large, clearly labelled example figure lets the reader explore its source, meaning and context. Three concise steps lead into a media-kit illustration, useful questions and a strong demo/contact closing. Best fit when the page should explain why the information is useful before anything else.

2. **Creator first — `/lab/data-trust/2`.** A warmer, more visual progression connects the person, account information and introduction. Larger miniatures explain connections, permissions and the media kit. Best fit when approaching the topic through creators and relationships feels most natural to Foam.

3. **Answers first — `/lab/data-trust/3`.** An editorial introduction leads directly to three visible questions and an interactive illustrated answer panel. Privacy and demonstration-data explanations stay visible below. Best fit for visitors arriving with a particular concern who want to find the relevant answer quickly.

All three retain clear commercial CTAs, distinguish fictional demonstration creators and figures from live results, and keep policy links accessible. They are alternatives for review, not three pages intended for the main navigation.

## Scope and next decision

These are isolated, one-off Lab previews. Existing public pages remain unchanged. They are deliberately excluded from the public website asset-usage inventory, consistent with other saved design concepts. If a direction is adopted publicly, its final assets and placements should then enter that inventory.

Choose the strongest hierarchy first; useful details can be combined afterwards. Before adopting more specific trust copy, confirm refresh behaviour, connection permissions and any new privacy/security assurances with the Foam team. The current designs do not depend on inventing those assurances.
